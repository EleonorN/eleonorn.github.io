Weather-driven ice cream marketing

How search data and weather data can shape an ice cream brand's marketing all year round, from weather-based ads in summer to a seasonal product in autumn.

Role Data analysis, content strategy
Tools Google Trends, SMHI open data, Google Sheets, Looker Studio, Chart.js
Context Growth Marketing, Berghs
Year 2026

The challenge

Ice cream is strongly linked to summer. The question was how an ice cream brand could use data to understand when interest drops, and how to keep people interested in ice cream when the season turns.

My approach

I combined two datasets: monthly search interest for "glass" (ice cream) in Sweden from Google Trends, and monthly average temperature in Stockholm from SMHI. I merged the data in Google Sheets and visualised it in Looker Studio.

I then compared ice cream searches with searches for "kladdkaka", a Swedish chocolate cake, to see what people search for instead when ice cream interest falls.

The insight

Ice cream searches follow the temperature closely. They rise in spring, peak in July and drop in autumn. The warmest month in the period, July 2025, also had the most searches.

When ice cream interest falls, searches for kladdkaka rise and peak every November. At the same time, ice cream searches in November have grown every year, and in November 2025 ice cream passed kladdkaka for the first time.

November 2021

Ice cream 27 · Kladdkaka 38

November 2025

Ice cream 38 · Kladdkaka 37

The recommendation

Spring and summer, weather-driven ads: connect live weather data from SMHI to the ad platform. Sunny days show ads with ice cream outdoors. Rainy days pause those ads and show a cosy indoor setting instead.

Autumn, a seasonal product: launch "Höstbitar", small chocolate-covered bites of kladdkaka and vanilla ice cream shaped as pumpkins and ghosts. The product combines ice cream with something people already search for in autumn.

The launch is supported by a Reel and an Instagram poll aimed at parents of young children, followed by a PR pitch to food journalists built on the Google Trends data.

Search interest is shown as an index from 0 to 100, not as the number of searches. The product and brand are a concept.
